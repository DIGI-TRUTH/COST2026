The scientific focus

Legitimation in context

Sentiment, circulation and argumentative form provide complementary signals. The planning framework connects them to community-specific repertoires, multimodal interpretation and comparative validation.

Relative repertoire compatibility is distinguished from observed acceptance. Predictive validity and multilingual comparability remain questions to test.

The network mechanism

Transfer through mixed packages

Each mixed transfer package is a capacity-building pathway: institutions teach some skills and learn others. Six pathways combine complementary competences through mobility, mentoring and four Training Schools; all begin in Year 1.

Four Working Groups, with six leaders/co-leaders in total, coordinate science. Dedicated management support and explicit scientific, training and monitoring functions organise delivery; later activities invite former trainees to co-teach.

From evidence to public use

A staged multilingual protocol, comparative cases and team-supported pre-bunking experiments inform a Code of Conduct, policy roadmap and three briefs. WG4 prepares illustrated claim cards, captioned videos and plain-language summaries for the portal, partner newsletters and teaching settings. Open briefings and targeted invitations seek practitioner and policy feedback; institutional attendance or endorsement is not assumed.

The policy pathway is relevant to the Digital Services Act, the European Media Freedom Act and the European Democracy Shield. Staffing, resources and the final operational language list require confirmation.

Comparative learning and evidence

Common comparative designs, practitioner and public feedback, reusable teaching materials and evidence gates strengthen the existing plan. Monitoring separates reach, learning and documented use.

Partnerships and professional expertise

Brazil — The University of São Paulo (USP) is envisaged as a hub connecting the network with Latin American research communities through contextual comparison, reciprocal training and knowledge exchange.

Jordan — Jordan Radio and Television Corporation (Production Department) is listed in the current proposal. Its planned data-analysis contribution links to WG2 — Data Framework and Cross-Validation; specific analytical skills and availability remain to be confirmed.

Technology partners such as moOngy S.A. and moOngy Labs can connect applied research and engineering with method validation and operational feasibility. Media and civil-society partners contribute use contexts and feedback. Academic qualifications are complemented, for some members, by more than 25 years of prior professional experience, including large-scale project delivery, technical-team management and Horizon-funded collaborations. This experience supports implementation, risk management and mentoring.
