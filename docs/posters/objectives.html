Scientific and capacity-building objectives

Research coordination, reciprocal learning, mitigation and measurable targets.

← Back to atlasPlan revised 19 October 2026

Objectives from the revised proposal. Quantitative targets require confirmed team capacity, resources and the final language list; they are not counts of confirmed participation.

The Action's aim is a common, multilingual and validated way of describing how post-truth, populist and polarising claims gain legitimacy online, and of translating that knowledge into mitigation. The research itself is carried out with the participating teams' own resources; the Action coordinates, validates and transfers.

Research coordination objectives

RCO1 – Common framework and protocol (WG1). Agree on a shared conceptual framework and multilingual glossary (M6). Release a common annotation protocol covering argument scheme and fallacy, legitimation repertoire, stance, the anchoring of comments to the claim, and image–comment reading: version 1.0 at M9 and version 2.0 at M24.

The planning target is annotation and validation in at least eight languages by M24, developed in two stages: an initial pilot in at least four languages, followed by replication and extension. This sequence balances early comparability with broader testing. Languages are selected by M3 using documented team capacity, legally accessible material, two annotators per language and diversity of linguistic and political contexts. The final language list and feasibility must be confirmed by the submitting network; eight is a target, not a verified count of current coverage. Agreement is reported by language and annotation dimension; insufficient agreement triggers codebook revision and retraining before pooled comparisons.

RCO2 – Comparable measurement and cross-validation (WG2). Agree on a common data and metadata model for federated, legally accessed data that remain with the teams holding them (M12). Cross-validate at least four families of existing methods against the protocol (M24, updated M36):

sentiment and emotion analysis;

network analysis and community detection;

non-binary (fuzzy) polarisation measures;

language-model-assisted coding with human validation.

Relative legitimation scores will be reported together with their absolute values and evidence coverage. They will be tested against observed reception wherever such data exist.

Targets: validation report per method family and language; versioned open repository (M30).

RCO3 – Comparative evidence in electoral cycles (WG3). Agree on a common design (M15) for at least six coordinated comparative case studies across four domains (gender equality, migration, climate policy and trust in science). Use the same design for small-scale pre-bunking experiments run by at least three teams. Data collection and experiments are funded by the teams.

Targets: comparative synthesis (M30); experiment report (M36).

RCO4 – From evidence to action (WG4). Translate comparable evidence into a consensus-based (Delphi) set of recommendations and a European Code of Conduct for digital integrity in electoral cycles (M36), a policy roadmap on DSA and EMFA implementation (M42) and three policy briefs (M48). Produce public "anatomies" of recurring claims for fact-checkers, journalists and educators. Mitigation combines repertoire-sensitive pre-bunking and dialogue guidance: teams co-design messages with practitioners, test them using a common protocol and report null or adverse effects alongside benefits. WG4 translates assessed findings into the Code and roadmap; effectiveness is not assumed in advance.

Capacity-building objectives

CBO1 – Transfer of competences from Year 1. Six mixed transfer packages (capacity-building pathways combining complementary skills and reciprocal teaching) each combine two or three related competences. Each package is co-led by two or three nodes from different countries, at least one of them in an Inclusiveness Target Country (ITC). They circulate competences through Short-Term Scientific Missions (STSMs) and Virtual Mobility from the first semester, and through four Training Schools (M9–12, M18–21, M30, M40).

Targets: 30–35 STSMs and at least 20 Virtual Mobility grants; every participating country with an active role in a package from Year 1; at least 10 former trainees co-teaching in the third and fourth Training Schools.

CBO2 – Bridging computational and social science. The six WG leaders/co-leaders across four WGs jointly cover computational and social-science expertise. Joint scientific coordination and paired STSMs connect both profiles within every WG; this does not require two appointed leaders in each WG.

CBO3 – Reach beyond the listed members. Training Schools are hybrid, and local hubs at participating institutions involve their postgraduate groups.

Targets: at least 150 Young Researchers and Innovators (YRIs) reached, counted as unique individuals; at least 70 of them with direct support.

The objectives are achievable because they build on existing team capacities (Section 1.E). They are concrete: every target has a month and an indicator in the progress panel (Section 3). They are ambitious because no common standard for measuring legitimation, rather than detection, exists across European languages.
